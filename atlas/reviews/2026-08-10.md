# Design review — 2026-08-10

The second standing review, run one day after the first, against a batch nobody
had judged: the transport (the century, run under the whole atlas), the
consensus layer (2,204 films described against a 59-term vocabulary, with a
decoy probe), the FILMGRAB frame measures, and a typed search that turns a
sentence into clauses.

Built fresh:

```
public/atlas.html  9450 KB — 2204 films, 22217 edges, 2204 placed
44 strata re-formed — 10140 film placements, 68 KB
28 registers, 28 of them with their own baked constellation
transport 1916-2026 — 1982 measured prints, descent 233/315 directed at 94.0%
forward, rebuttal 110/110 at 88.2% back, 343 pens, 4 films under the residual
floor, 23 KB
```

`driver.mjs smoke` PASSED — wall 240 tiles, map 25 nodes / 6 curves, sky ink
9.58%, silver print 194 at 16.38%, passage 8 hops / 8 claims in 26 ms, console
clean. `transport-probe.mjs` PASSED all seven sections independently.
`driver.mjs film transport` PASSED — 194 frames over 6,921 ms, peak share 7.30%,
half the motion spread over 52 frames, 0 identical frames inside the run.

Everything below was then measured in real Chromium at 1440×900, 900×820 and
390×780. Probe scripts are scratch, not deliverable. **Posters do not resolve in
this container**, so every screenshot shows the generated cell — which AGENTS
rule 9 says is the visual identity, but it is not what a visitor with a network
sees, and nothing about poster imagery is judged below.

Judged against the same two sentences:

> "a world that belongs to the user who plays with it… not just an atlas, but
> THEIR atlas. A world filled with films that they love, and importantly, films
> they're going to love."

> "the constellations are the attractor, and the insights are why they stay."

The aperture fix in particular is the review working. The regression it caught
has not returned, and the check that caught it — a probe that asks the page what
it *drew* rather than the function what it *would* draw — is now the house style.
`transport-probe.mjs` instruments the 2D context and counts stroked segments with
their dash state, filled arcs, and sprite blits with their alpha and size. It
compares the resting canvas byte for byte, 495 KB of PNG, against the same page
with the transport switched off. Eight negative controls, 8 of 8 fire the check
they are aimed at. That is the correct instrument and it is honestly built.

Which makes the rest of this review a report on where that instrument still
cannot see.

## The first thirty seconds

Cold at 1440×900, the front page is unchanged and still the best-composed thing
here: wordmark, search, three buttons, *Every film is downstream of another*, a
paragraph that actually explains the product, 240 films. One click to
`CONSTELLATION` and the atlas is a 383×389 ball centred in a 584×427 aperture —
100% of 2,204 films inside the print at all three viewports, filling 66% of its
width and 91% of its height. That is the composition the last review said the
bug was hiding, and it is worth the trip.

Then your eye goes to the new thing along the bottom, and the first sentence the
century says to you is **`2026 · THE REEL HAS RUN`**. You have run nothing. It is
a state label written from the machine's point of view, sitting where a title
should be, at 8.5 px. Press `RUN THE CENTURY` and the atlas fills in from three
films in 1920 to 2,204 in 2026 over seven seconds, and it is genuinely lovely —
194 presented frames, motion spread across 52 of them, no single frame carrying
more than 7.3% of it. Nobody else has this.

And then you hold a year, and the whole thing comes apart. That is finding 1.

## Findings, ranked by how much they matter

### 1. Holding a year shrinks the print to 55% of its area — and the year falls out of it — BREAKS IT

The transport's promise, in its own words: *"The atlas as it stood in 1930.
Nothing has moved; the rest has not been made yet."* Measured, at 1440×900,
what actually happens when you touch the track:

| | readout height | aperture | area |
|---|---|---|---|
| at rest | 155 px | 584 × 427 at (428, 153) | — |
| holding 1958 | **264 px** | **435 × 318 at (502, 153)** | **55%** |

The readout grows 70% taller the moment a year is held, the safe band shrinks
with it, and `skyGate()` correctly inscribes a smaller Academy rectangle in what
is left. Every number in that chain is right. The result is that the print gets
45% smaller exactly when you start looking at it.

Then the films fall out of the smaller print. Positions are fixed by design — a
film does not move when the century runs — so the born set is a lopsided subset
of a ball composed for the whole corpus. Measured, born-film screen positions
against the live `sky.gate` at 1440×900:

| year | born | inside the aperture | born bbox vs aperture (W/H) | centroid offset from aperture centre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1920 | 3 | **33%** | 14% / 40% | (−81, +128) px |
| 1930 | 55 | **60%** | 78% / 59% | (−75, +145) px |
| 1945 | 138 | 68% | 79% / **103%** | (−63, +122) px |
| 1960 | 484 | 75% | 87% / **112%** | (−44, +93) px |
| 1975 | 1051 | 84% | 87% / **123%** | (−39, +59) px |
| 1990 | 1477 | 86% | 87% / **123%** | (−26, +51) px |
| 2005 | 1843 | 86% | 88% / **123%** | (−12, +48) px |
| 2026 | 2204 | **100%** | 66% / 91% | (−1, −5) px |

From 1945 to 2005 the films being made are **taller than the frame they are
being made in**. Two in five of 1930's films are outside the print entirely. The
one cursor where the composition is correct is the one where nothing is
happening.

Look at `/tmp/judge-tp-1930.png` and the argument is over: 1930 is a small green
scribble hanging off the bottom-left corner of an aperture it does not occupy,
under a 55-word paragraph about standard deviations. This is the single most
beautiful idea in the build rendered as a rendering fault.

**Why the probes could not see it.** `transport-probe.mjs` checks that no film
is drawn before its year (11,487 discs matched back at 11 cursors), that no
connection reaches an unborn film (14,209 stroked endpoints), that the strike is
a function of age alone, that sub-0.5 claims stay dashed at 11 cursors, and that
0 of 2,204 films sit under the track at three viewports. All true. None of them
asks the only question a reader asks: **is the year I am holding in the frame.**

### 2. The century names a hue to one decimal place and never names a film — BREAKS THE STATED DIRECTION

Graze 1954. Verbatim, the whole readout:

> **1954** · 130 FILMS WITHIN TWO YEARS OF 1954 · SHOWN WHERE THEY ARE
> ◐ AMPLIFIED · THE LIGHT IS THE MEASURED MEAN OF THIS YEAR'S POSTERS — HUE
> 49.3° FROM 110 PRINTS, CHROMA ×3.6, AUTHORED. THE DECADE SEPARATES AT 4.99 SD
> AND EXPLAINS 2.1% OF ONE FILM'S OWN COLOUR, SO IT LIGHTS THE ROOM AND NEVER A
> DISC — AND A YEAR IS READ AS A WASH OVER THE WHOLE PLATE, NEVER OFF ONE FILM'S
> POSITION (RMSE 20.9 YEARS AGAINST A CORPUS SD OF 23.4).
> … The other three types carry a recorded direction on 31 of 21,792 edges, so
> they are drawn from the middle out to both ends rather than given an arrow
> nobody measured.

The corpus contains 24 films made in 1954. They are Seven Samurai, Rear Window,
Sansho the Bailiff, La Strada, Johnny Guitar, Diabolique, Dial M for Murder,
Late Chrysanthemums, The Crucified Lovers, Magnificent Obsession, and fourteen
more. **Not one of them is named.** The reader is given an RMSE instead.

The paragraph is byte-identical at 1930, 1968, 1999 and 1954 apart from two
numbers. It is a methods footnote that has been promoted to the primary caption
and then left there permanently, in caps, at 8.5–9.5 px, in the box that is also
the reason the print shrank in finding 1. AGENTS rule 8 and DESIGN's colour-tier
system are right and this is not what they asked for: `◐ amplified` is the
honest word, and the honest word is two syllables. The other fifty-three are the
project talking to itself.

DESIGN.md already knows this. From the last review's finding 11: *"slate
lettering is doing the job of prose."* The resting readout took that note —
*"Every film is up there, placed by what it is joined to and nothing else… Two
films in five are behind no world at all"* is a real sentence written for a
person. The transport readout did not.

Findings 1 and 2 are one edit. Cut the colour note to its first clause, put the
year's films in the space it frees, and the aperture comes back on its own.

### 3. The layer that answers this project's own open problem #2 is on disk and reaches no visitor — BREAKS THE STATED DIRECTION

STATE.md, open problem 2: *"1,324 of 2,204 films carry no reading at all. That
is where the trivia share is hiding."* Measured against the shipped corpus
today: **1,186 of 2,204 films touch no authored claim.**

`pipeline/out/consensus.json` — 955 KB, generated in this batch, verified by me
against its own file — holds an entry for **all 1,186** of them, and a concrete,
specific, checkable one-line recall for **663**:

- *1941* → `well` · "the Ferris wheel breaks loose and rolls off the pier into the ocean, still lit"
- *Suspiria* → `well` · "Susie's dance in the mirror room snaps Olga's body apart in the next studio"
- *Dune* → `well` · "the Guild Navigator unfolding in its tank of orange gas; 'the sleeper must awaken'"
- *8½* → `well` · "the opening traffic-jam dream: he floats up out of the car and is tugged down by a rope on his ankle"

Corpus-wide the file checks out exactly as reported: 1,640 `well` / 458
`outline` / 106 `no`, 2,098 answered of 2,204, and the decoy probe reads
`decoyTally {well:0, outline:0, no:24}` against `realTally {well:69, outline:26,
no:1}`. It is real work and the honesty discipline around it is real.

**And `grep -c consensus app/template.html` returns 0, and `app/build.js` never
opens the file.** Nor does anything else in `app/`.

The full inventory of instruments built and not shipped:

| | |
|---|---|
| `pipeline/consensus.js` + `axes.js` + `consensus-decoy.js` + measures | the 59-attribute layer, 955 KB of answers |
| `pipeline/match.js`, `questioner.js`, `query-parse.js`, `query-lexicon.json` | the typed search — 548 phrases, all 59 attributes reachable |
| `app/layout-match.js` | 584 lines, **zero references anywhere in the repo** |
| `pipeline/out/frame-measures.json` | 1,017 films × 7 OKLab axes measured from curator-selected *frames* — the file's own note calls film-grab "the truer instrument" — **zero consumers anywhere in the repo** |

**5,211 lines of code and roughly 1.3 MB of measured per-film data that no
visitor can reach.** `match.js`, `questioner.js` and `query-parse.js` reference
only each other and their own measurement scripts: a closed loop that never
touches `app/`.

I want to be precise about the criticism, because the work is good. The problem
is not that a prototype exists. The problem is that this batch's answer to "the
insights are why they stay" was to build a fourth insight layer while the first
one — 1,186 films with nothing to say about them — went unshipped, and the
picture a visitor sees gained a track and a paragraph about chroma gain.

### 4. Love five films and the constellation does not notice — DULLS IT, AND IT IS THE OWNER'S SENTENCE

Marked five films (Vertigo, Stalker, In the Mood for Love, Come and See,
Persona) through the app's own `LOVE IT` button, confirmed in
`localStorage["atlas-preferences-v1"]`, header reads `FOR YOU 5`. Then `#/sky`.
The readout, verbatim:

> THE WHOLE ATLAS · 2204 FILMS · 22217 CONNECTIONS · … Every film is up there,
> placed by what it is joined to and nothing else. Start with one of the 28
> worlds along the top, run the century on the track below, or pick any film to
> open its map. Two films in five are behind no world at all.

That is **byte-identical to the cold-load text**. Five gold rings are on the
canvas — I can count them in `/tmp/judge-sky-loved.png` — and nothing names
them, nothing counts them, nothing takes you to them, and the atlas does not
compose around them. Hold 1968 with those five marked and the century will not
tell you which of yours existed yet.

The wall does better: "Because you loved Persona · 12 SUGGESTIONS" is a real
shelf and the picks are not degree-biased (median corpus rank 380, one pick at
rank 2116, min degree 13 — rule 1 holds). But it names only the most recently
marked of five, and each tile carries a relationship name — `2001 · DESCENDS
FROM` — where the claim would be.

The apparatus of "THEIR atlas" is a gold ring and a counter in the header. The
attractor is still everybody's.

### 5. The 28 sentences that say what a world *is* are native browser tooltips — DULLS IT

`registers.json` carries a blurb per world, and they are the best short prose in
the project:

- gothic — *"Old stone, inherited guilt, and something alive in the west wing."*
- cold-science-fiction — *"Space treated as a philosophical problem, not an action set."*
- folk-horror — *"Dread grown out of a place, and the things the people there still believe."*
- melancholy-drift — *"Nothing is resolved. The film keeps moving anyway."*

They ship into the artifact. They appear in exactly two places:
`app/template.html:4841` — `b.title = def.blurb || ""` — and inside the tile's
aria-label at 4858. That is a native tooltip after a second of hover, and an
aria-label. **On touch they do not exist at all.**

Meanwhile `#/sky/world:gothic` — the world's own page, arrived at deliberately —
prints its tier note and the placement caveat and nothing about gothic cinema.
A reader who commits to a world learns less about it than a reader who hovers
and waits.

### 6. 273 readings are still drawn with a record's authority — DULLS IT

Unchanged from the last review, and now counted exactly from the shipped corpus:

The radial map's laid-out footprint is **707 px wide at 1440×900 and 696 px at

### 10. `THE REEL HAS RUN`, before anyone has run anything — POLISH

The track's mode label on cold arrival. It is the machine's state, not the
reader's. DESIGN's framing — the resting picture is the last frame of a reel
that has already run — is a lovely idea and this is the wrong four words for it,
in the wrong slot, at 8.5 px. The whole track chrome sits at the project's type
floor: decade labels at 8 px, mode and hint at 8.5 px. Contrast is fine
(5.09:1 worst measured, on `#0F0D0D`-composited ground); size is not.

### 11. The search prototype's picture is a rainbow doughnut with a hole where the answers are — POLISH, but pointed at `template.html`

Run in real Chromium against its own artifact. The mechanism is good and the
legibility claim holds: the owner's five-clause sentence produces five chips,
each carrying the reader's own span beside our term with an × on it, plus
`THE MATRIX READS AS 1 MOOD TERM HERE, SO ITS TONE AND TEXTURE WERE READ TOO`.
That is the right idea, correctly built, and the honest `NO FILM ANSWERS ALL OF
IT — THE DISTANCE IS CARRYING THE QUERY` is exactly this project's voice.

The picture is not. 2,204 dots in a categorical rainbow — a palette the atlas
has never used and whose whole premise (colour is measured, or it says it is
authored) it contradicts — arranged as an annulus with an **empty centre**,
where the centre is supposed to be where the best answers are. The strongest
result is drawn as a hole. `INTEGRATION.md` names where this goes in
`template.html`. It should not go there looking like this.

## The rules, spot-checked

**Rule 1 — nothing encodes popularity.** Three places it could fail, all clean.
`transport-probe` measures the strike as a function of age alone: 5 age groups
on screen at once, each carrying exactly one alpha and one size across degrees
1–34. The wall's median shown degree is 21 against a corpus median of 20 and it
reaches rank 2203. The recommender's 12 picks for five loved films have a median
corpus degree-rank of 380, include a film at rank 2116, and bottom out at degree
13. No degree term appears in the sky's disc sizing.

**Rule 4 — captions never blur.** Every node on `#/film/vertigo`, at every ring
position: `filter: none`, `opacity: 1`, `color: rgb(240,234,224)`. Sharp.

**Rule 3 — sub-0.5 dashed.** 11 cursors across the century, dashed count equals
what the page's own data says must be dashed, every time. Holds.

**Rule 8 — authored colour says it is authored.** Holds, and then some — see
finding 2, where it says so at 55 words.

## Coherence

The count of simultaneous visual languages went up again: the transport adds a
census histogram, a playhead, a strike curve, a year wash and two graft marks.
It coheres better than it has any right to, because the track is disciplined by
the same idea as everything else — it is chrome the camera fits above, it costs
nothing at rest (1.01× the control, 0 idle draws in 3 s), and the resting canvas
is byte-identical with it switched off. That last proof is the most rigorous
thing in the repo.

But the deeper incoherence the last review named has got worse, not better:
**the insights are not in the picture, and this batch put three more layers of
insight outside it.** The constellation shows five relation colours and no
words. The map on arrival shows six relationship names and no claims. The
transport shows a hue and no films. The consensus layer shows nothing to anyone.
Four views, and the only place a reader meets a sentence about a film is a panel
they have to open.

One number makes the shape of it plain. This batch shipped a transport that
prints a year's mean poster hue to one decimal — and did not ship a file that
knows what happens in 663 films the atlas currently has nothing to say about.

## The three things I would fix first

1. **The transport readout.** Findings 1 and 2, which are one edit. Cut the
   colour note to `◐ amplified — the year's own prints, chroma raised` and move
   the rest behind the fold that already exists. That gives the readout back
   ~110 px, which gives the aperture back its 584×427, which puts 1930 back
   inside the print. Then spend the freed space on **the films of that year** —
   four or five titles, which the corpus already has (24 for 1954) and which
   cost nothing to look up. The century currently runs and says nothing. It
   should be able to say *Seven Samurai. Rear Window. Sansho the Bailiff.*

2. **Ship one field of the consensus layer.** Not the search, not the
   questioner, not `layout-match.js` — one field. Put the `recall` line in the
   film panel, under the claims, marked with its own tier the way the registers
   already mark theirs. That is 663 films that currently have nothing said about
   them getting one specific, checkable sentence each, and it is the direct
   answer to STATE.md's own open problem 2. Everything else in that 5,211 lines
   can wait; this is one string per film and it is already generated and already
   decoy-tested.

3. **Make the constellation know the reader.** Five gold rings and a header
   counter is not "THEIR atlas". The readout knows how to say *"Two films in
   five are behind no world at all"* — it can say *"Five of these are yours"*
   with a way to reach them, and the transport can strike them differently as
   the century passes their year. This is the owner's first sentence and it is
   the one surface that has not moved in two reviews.

Runner-up, four characters of markup and the highest ratio of quality to effort
in this document: **put the world's blurb on the world's page.** *"Old stone,
inherited guilt, and something alive in the west wing"* is currently a
`title=""` attribute.

## Is the site becoming more or less itself?

**More, and by a wider margin than yesterday — but it is now unmistakably two
projects, and only one of them is a site.**

The thing on screen got a century. Not a timeline widget: an actual reel, where
a film appears in the year it was made and a connection travels in the direction
its own type was recorded to argue, and where the resting picture is provably —
byte for byte, 495 KB of PNG — the picture that shipped before it. The
engineering honesty around it is the best in the repo: the prototype's headline
"80.6% forward" was found to be a bug and corrected down to "94.0% of the 233
descents that carry a direction, and here is the denominator"; the colour note
was demoted from `● RECORDED` to `◐ amplified` because one tier of the claim was
authored. Nobody made this project do that. It did it to itself, and that is
what being itself looks like.

**On the first standing criticism — building faster than it is looking — the
answer is: the looking got much better, and it is still one level behind the
building.** Last review's example was a check that measured the model rather
than the picture; that is fixed, comprehensively. `transport-probe.mjs`
instruments the canvas context, compares real pixels, and proves eight negative
controls fire. The aperture regression did not recur. But the checks now
establish that the picture's *mechanism* is right — nothing before its time,
one alpha per age, nothing under the track — and still nothing asks whether the
picture is *composed*. So the frame shrinks by 45% and the year falls out of it
and eleven cursors of measurement report PASS. The gap has moved from "did it
render" to "is it any good to look at", which is real progress and is still a
gap. The next probe this project needs is the dullest one imaginable: at every
cursor, what fraction of what exists is inside the print.

**On the second — more beautiful faster than more personal — it got worse, and
this is now the defining problem.** Since yesterday the project added four
instruments: a transport, a 59-attribute consensus layer, a frame-colour layer
and a typed search. Three of the four reach no visitor at all. The fourth
reaches every visitor and does not know who they are. Against that, the personal
layer gained one genuine thing — "Mark a film you love / THEN THIS FILLS UP",
and a suggestion shelf that works and is not degree-biased. That is a real
improvement and it is one shelf on the front page. Mark five films you love and
the constellation, the transport, the worlds and Passage are all exactly as they
were.

The uncomfortable version: this project is better at proving things about films
than at telling anyone about them. It can tell you that 1954's prints sit at hue
49.3° across 110 measured posters with an RMSE of 20.9 years against a corpus SD
of 23.4. It cannot tell you that Seven Samurai came out that year. Both of those
sentences are about the same twenty-four films, and only one of them is why
anyone stays.

Fix the readout tonight. Then ship one sentence per film.
