# Design review — 2026-08-09

The first standing review, run at the end of the largest single day this project
has had: the worlds strip, the re-forming atlas, the night sky and meteors, the
darker ground, the folding readout, the map arriving on the map, outside-click
dismissal, addressable worlds, the Latent Image gate, and 28 registers all
landed inside twenty-four hours. Nobody had stepped back and looked at the whole
since.

Built fresh (`node atlas/app/build.js --out public/atlas.html`, 9,348 KB, 2,204
films, 22,217 edges, 44 strata, 28 registers). `driver.mjs smoke` PASSED — wall
240 tiles, map 25 nodes / 6 curves, sky ink 13.21%, silver print 194 at 16.36%,
passage 8 hops in 83 ms, console clean. Everything below was then measured in
real Chromium at 1440×900, 900×820 and 390×780, panel open and closed. Probes
are in `.judge/` (gitignored scratch); they are diagnostic, not deliverable.

Judged against two sentences of the owner's:

> "a world that belongs to the user who plays with it… not just an atlas, but
> THEIR atlas. A world filled with films that they love, and importantly, films
> they're going to love."

> "the constellations are the attractor, and the insights are why they stay."

---

## The first thirty seconds

Cold, at 1440×900: a wordmark, a search field, three buttons, a serif hero —
*Every film is downstream of another* — a paragraph that actually explains the
product, and a wall of 240 films. It is composed, warm and confident, and it
does not look like anything else. Nothing here needs defending.

The attractor is one click away and mislabelled. The strongest thing in the
build is the constellation, and the front page offers it as an 8.5px tracked
underline, `SEE THE WHOLE ATLAS AT ONCE`, sitting below the lede at the same
weight as `PRESS / TO SEARCH`. The button in the header says `CONSTELLATION`,
which names a control, not a reason.

Once you are there, the attractor attracts — 2,204 discs, a strip of 28
coloured worlds along the top, a legend that names five kinds of argument. And
the insights are three gestures away and, on arrival at a film, entirely
off screen. That gap is the shape of most of what follows.

---

## Findings, ranked by how much they matter

### 1. The aperture is wrong on every ordinary route into the whole atlas — BREAKS IT

`skyGate()` inscribes a 1.37 Academy rectangle in the safe band, and DESIGN.md
says of it: *"THE CHROME IS OUTSIDE THE APERTURE BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by
a second set of numbers that could disagree with the first."* In the shipped
build the two sets of numbers disagree, badly.

Measured, `sky.gate` (what is drawn) against `skyGate()` (what it should be),
after a full 3.2 s settle:

| route in | drawn | correct | drift |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold load `#/sky` | 1004×733 at y=0 | 687×502 at y=153 | **317 px** |
| wall → `CONSTELLATION` | 1004×733 at y=0 | 687×502 at y=153 | **317 px** |
| wall → *see the whole atlas at once* | 1004×733 at y=0 | 687×502 at y=153 | **317 px** |
| cold load `#/sky/world:gothic` | 760×555 at y=33 | 760×555 at y=33 | 0 |

Every path a reader actually takes to the whole atlas gets the broken one. At
900×820 the drift is 294 px, at 390×780 it is 176 px. It corrects itself only
after the reader chooses a world and comes back.

The cause is one line, added today in `1bffef2`:

```js
if (!sky.chrome || !sky.gate) skyGateApply();
```

The gate is now recomputed only when the chrome cache is cold. On the first
paint the band has not been measured yet — the strip and the readout are not up
— so the gate is computed against `top=0, bot=733`, the cache goes warm, and it
is never computed again. The perf reasoning in the comment is sound. The guard
is keyed to the wrong thing.

What it costs is not abstract. Patching the guard out and re-rendering the same
page: the aperture drops to 687×502, its top edge lands just under the strip and
its bottom just over the readout, and the atlas — which is a 455×458 ball either
way, the camera does not move — goes from filling 45% of the aperture's width to
66%. The whole-atlas view stops reading as a dot in a black room and starts
reading as a print in a gate. **The complaint "the constellation looks small and
lost at desktop" is not a composition decision anybody made. It is this bug.**

The gate is also today's most conceptually load-bearing new object — the print
in the projector — and it currently renders as a vertical band with its top edge
hidden behind the strip and its bottom edge off the bottom of the canvas. That
is not an aperture. It is a rectangle.

### 2. The worlds strip covers the Passage route's own title and its first claim — BREAKS IT

Passage is the best writing in the product. Eight specific claims routing 8½ to
*Prince of Darkness*, each one a sentence you could say out loud. And the top of
that panel is underneath the newest piece of chrome.

Measured overlap of `#sky-worlds` (z-index 9) over `#sky-readbox` (z-index
auto):

| | overlap |
|---|---|
| 1440×900 | **520 × 115 px** |
| 900×820 | **632 × 139 px** |
| 390×780 | **366 × 134 px** |

The hidden text is, verbatim, `Passage · 8½ → Prince of Darkness · 8 crossings`
plus the opening of the first claim. A reader arriving at `#/passage/a/b` from a
link sees a list of unattributed sentences beginning mid-thought, with no title
and no endpoints.

The rule that produced this is right and was applied one case short: the strip
is open *iff the atlas is whole*, and during a Passage the atlas is whole. But a
route is a selection of a different kind, and 28 doors to somewhere else are not
what a reader wants over the top of the chain they just asked for.

Total opaque chrome as a share of the viewport, for context: 48.3% at 1440×900,
81.1% at 900×820, **116% at 390×780** — over 100% because the boxes now overlap
each other.

### 3. The front page and *Surprise me* are a degree ranking — BREAKS THE STATED DIRECTION

```js
const WALL_ORDER = KEYS.slice().sort((a,b)=>
     (ADJ[b].length - ADJ[a].length)
  || ((F[b].poster?1:0) - (F[a].poster?1:0))
  || (BEST_BOND[b] - BEST_BOND[a])
  || (a < b ? -1 : 1));
```

The first sort key is node degree. DESIGN.md's wall section fixed the poster
handicap and the year tiebreak — both correctly, both for exactly the right
reason — and never looked at the primary key.

Measured:

- rank vs degree across all 2,204: Pearson **−0.679**
- front page mean degree **24.19**, the other 1,964 films **19.67**
- the front page's degree floor is **22**; only **374 of 2,204 films (17%)**
  reach it
- **1,222 films sit at exactly degree 20** and are separated only by whether
  they have a poster
- `Surprise me` draws from `WALL_ORDER.slice(0, 260)` — mean degree **24.0**.
  **The one button in this product labelled as serendipity cannot show you a
  film outside the best-connected 11.8% of the corpus.**

AGENTS rule 1 is written about edge distance, so this is not a literal breach.
It is the founding correction's own sentence — *"a degree-weighted graph
collapses toward the canon and confirms what the user already knows"* — enacted
on the front door and on the discovery button. Against "films they're going to
love", it is the single largest miss in the build. 83% of the corpus is
browsable only through search or by dragging around a field of 2,204 identical
dots.

### 4. In Passage the films vanish and the arguments do not — DULLS IT

`skyDraw`, disc pass:

```js
ctx.globalAlpha = (hasRoute ? 0.07 : inPreview ? 0.82 : 0.16) * a;
```

The edge pass, 140 lines earlier, has no `hasRoute` term at all. So during a
route every film off the route is dimmed 12× and every *line* stays at full
weight. Measured on the composited canvas: ink 13.99% → **5.75%**, bright pixels
13.40% → **5.17%**. On screen the atlas becomes a grey cobweb with eight lit
dots in it.

This is the rack focus running backwards. The signature says imagery recedes
with graph distance and the *subject* stays sharp; here the subject is erased
and the connective tissue survives. The correction already exists, four
paragraphs up in the same function, for the strip preview:

```js
/* Grazing a frame on the strip is a rack focus onto a SET, so the lines have
   to fall back with the discs. Left at full weight they are the brightest
   thing on screen and the 47 films being previewed disappear inside their
   own atlas. */
if (sky.previewSet) alpha*=0.34;
```

The lesson was learned today, written down today, and not carried across to the
one older view that needs it.

### 5. The colour key is not a key — DULLS IT

DESIGN.md rejected chroma-gain amplification for a specific measured reason:
*"cyberpunk, cold science fiction and artificial life all resolved within 8° of
each other. Three doors that look identical is the failure the treatment layer
exists to fix."*

The shipped hues, read out of `static/registers.json`:

- **cyberpunk mood 204°, cold science fiction 206°, artificial life 208°** —
  within **4°**. The exact three registers, closer together than in the version
  that was rejected.
- **12 of 28 registers sit between 31° and 53°** — a 22° band of amber holding
  43% of the doors, where even spacing would give them 155°.
- **8 more sit between 197° and 215°.**
- Adjacent hue gaps: median **5°**, minimum **0°** (silver print / films about
  films at 40°, cyberpunk / haunted house one degree apart at 196–197°), maximum
  **85°** of empty ring.

Lightness and saturation carry some of the separation the hue does not — the
silver print at s5 and new wave at s22 l85 do read apart — but that is three
registers being rescued by a second channel, not a key.

Measured on the rendered miniatures themselves, thresholded to ink masks:
pairwise shape IoU median **0.548**, 25% of pairs above 0.60. So the frames
*do* differ in shape, more than I expected from looking at them — the strip's
argument for existing is real. It is simply not the channel a reader reads at
104×60 px. They read hue, and hue is a four-clump rainbow.

DESIGN's own words: *"Three doors that look identical is the failure the
treatment layer exists to fix."* There are twelve.

### 6. Twenty-eight honesty marks with no key — DULLS IT

The strip prints `● 194 FILMS`, `◐ 108 FILMS`, `○ 176 FILMS` on every frame.
Measured on the resting sky at 1440×900: **28 tier glyphs on screen**, and the
strings "recorded", "amplified" and "authored" appear **zero** times anywhere in
the visible text. The meaning arrives only after a reader commits to a world and
reads the readout.

The `aria-label` carries `Colour authored.` on every frame. So the screen-reader
path is better informed about the project's central honesty claim than the
picture is. That inversion is worth naming: this is the one project where the
honesty tier is not a footnote, and sighted readers get it as an undecoded
dingbat.

### 7. 273 readings are drawn with a record's authority — DULLS IT

```js
const isLowConfidence = e => Number.isFinite(e.confidence)
  ? e.confidence < 0.5 : e.source === "reading";
```

Of 1,162 `reading` edges, **889 draw dashed and 273 draw solid** — visually
indistinguishable from a record. **93 of those 273 sit at confidence exactly
0.50**, one hundredth of a point from being marked.

Meanwhile the constellation's legend captions the dashed swatch
`READING, NOT RECORD`, which is true of 76.5% of readings and false for the
other 23.5%. And in the panel — the surface where the claim text is actually
read — every claim row carries the same `solid 2px` rule whatever it is:

```
solid 2px rgb(201,135,63) || descends from · Mulholland Drive · reading, not record
solid 2px rgb(201,135,63) || shares a source · Diabolique
```

Only the label separates them. AGENTS rule 3 says the interface must never
launder a reading into apparent fact; rule 8 says record and reading are
different *kinds* of claim, not one confidence number. The dash channel is
keyed to confidence and captioned as if it were keyed to tier. Both rules are
being honoured somewhere and neither is honoured everywhere.

### 8. Nothing tells a reader how to make it theirs — DULLS IT, AND IT IS THE OWNER'S SENTENCE

The Latent Image is the best new idea in the build and I want to say so before
criticising it: at 40 marks the plate genuinely reads. Measured, marking 40
loved films moves mean composited chroma 4.32 → 6.20 and mean luma 149.6 →
150.0, and inked pixels move **0.000%** — the invariant holds, and the picture
of forty coloured stars in a silver field is the truest expression of "THEIR
atlas" anywhere in this product. Seen-only marks at the same count read too
(chroma 4.32 → 5.67), which I had doubted.

The route to it is closed:

- `markFilm()` is reachable from **two buttons three gestures deep** — open a
  film, click the centre poster, then `Seen it` / `Love it`.
- The `s` / `l` shortcuts DESIGN.md introduced precisely to fix that are
  **undiscoverable**: no hint, no `title`, no `aria-keyshortcuts`, no mention in
  any view's text. Measured on the panel: `Seen it title=- aria=-`.
- The constellation **never explains the plate**. DESIGN removed that sentence
  from the resting readout for a good measured reason (it changed the camera
  fit) and moved it to the film readout. The consequence is that a reader with
  40 marks opens the sky, sees 40 coloured dots in 2,164 silver ones, and is
  told nothing.
- The empty state says *"Mark a film you love / then this fills up"* and does
  not say how.
- `FOR YOU 0` is in the header on every screen. DESIGN's own rule is
  *"No denominator. Anywhere. Ever… The existing 'For you n' header is the
  pattern not to extend."* It was not extended. It was also not removed, and it
  is the first thing the product says about the reader: zero.

### 9. The constellation gives a phone 57% of its own screen — DULLS IT

Opaque chrome as a share of the viewport, constellation at rest:

| | chrome |
|---|---|
| 1440×900 | 23.1% |
| 900×820 | 30.7% |
| 390×780 | **42.8%** |

The readout ships **unfolded** by default and is the largest of those boxes. The
fold exists, it is measured, it is remembered — and a first-time reader on a
phone never sees its benefit, because the default is the expensive state. On a
390×780 screen the header takes ~230 px, the strip 135 px and the readout ~205
px, leaving the attractor about a third of the screen.

Second-order: the strip shows **4 of 28 worlds** on a phone with no scroll
affordance beyond a half-cut fifth frame. Twenty-four doors are behind seven
screens of horizontal drag that nothing announces.

### 10. The panel is the one place a failed poster leaves a hole — DULLS IT

```js
// line 1985, wall + map tiles
onerror="this.outerHTML=genArt('${k}')"
// line 2757, the panel head
? `<img class="poster" src="${esc(f.poster)}" alt="" decoding="async">`
: `<div class="poster generated-poster">${genArt(k)}</div>`
```

A film with no poster gets its generated cell in the panel — correct, AGENTS 9.
A film whose poster *fails* gets nothing: the head collapses to `FILM / Vertigo
/ 1958 · Alfred Hitchcock` with an empty column beside it, which is what every
phone panel screenshot in this container shows. The wall and the map both
recover; the panel does not. One missing attribute.

### 11. Slate lettering is doing the job of prose — DULLS IT

The amplified readout, on screen, in 8.5px uppercase mono at .2em tracking:

> `◐ AMPLIFIED · THESE 59 FILMS' OWN POSTER COLOURS SIT 6.75 SD OFF THE CORPUS
> MEAN, TOWARD HUE 243 — THAT DIRECTION IS MEASURED, THIS INTENSITY IS CHOSEN`

That is 130 characters of argument set in the typeface DESIGN.md reserves for
"metadata, legend, labels". It is the most important sentence the interface
says — the one that makes this cartography rather than decoration — and it is
set in the hardest thing on the page to read. The text serif exists and is two
lines below it doing exactly this job.

### 12. Both maps waste the widest viewport — POLISH, and partly known

The radial map at 1440×900 occupies **49% of the stage width and 94% of its
height** (707×699 in a 1440×747 stage). The ellipse is portrait on a landscape
screen: vertically pinned, horizontally half empty. Open question 3 already owns
the 1024×660 case; this is the opposite complaint at the other end and nobody
owns it. It is why the desktop map reads as a diagram floating in a void while
the 900×820 version reads as a composition.

### 13. Smaller worlds get bigger discs — POLISH

`treatment.scale` correlates **−0.481** with register count (folk horror, 10
films, 1.12; something is out there, 70 films, 1.18; family as a trap, 194
films, 1.06). Inside a register it is one number for everybody, so rule 1 holds.
Across registers, disc size encodes rarity. That may well be the right call —
ten films in a frame need presence — but it is undocumented, and it sits in
direct tension with the density invariant DESIGN.md derives two sections
earlier.

### 14. `layout-match.js` is 510 lines wired to nothing — POLISH

The query sky commit (`1bffef2`) reads like a shipped feature: *"distance from
the centre is how well a film matched… A search can never come back empty."* The
only change to `template.html` in that commit is the gate perf guard that caused
finding 1. `layout-match.js` is referenced by `build.js`, the template and the
tests **zero** times. `static/fingerprints.json` (2,198 of 2,204 films) is not
consumed by the app either.

This is not a code-hygiene note. The query sky is the feature that would answer
"films they're going to love", it is designed, it is written, and it is not in
the product — and the commit log says otherwise. The record should say what
shipped.

### 15. Eight glass panels — POLISH

`backdrop-filter: blur(…)` appears 8 times: the header, the panel, the readout,
the stratum strip, three overlays. At 0.82–0.90 background alpha over near-black
it is nearly invisible, so this is not a visual complaint. It is the one
technique on the deliberately-avoided list that is in the file, and it earns
nothing at that alpha. Worth deleting on principle, or worth writing down as an
argued exception.

## The rules, spot-checked

**Rule 1 — nothing encodes popularity.** Three places checked. The
constellation's disc radius is one number for the whole field, verified.
Register `scale` is uniform within a register (finding 13 is the across-register
note). The wall order fails it in spirit (finding 3).

**Rule 4 — captions never blur.** Checked at three viewports on the radial map
and in the constellation with a film held: **0 blurred captions, 0 caption
overlaps, 0 label collisions.** The only `filter: blur()` in the file are
`backdrop-filter` on chrome and the `resolve` keyframe on an arriving poster.
Clean.

**Rule 8 — authored colour says it is authored.** In the readout, exemplary:
`○ AUTHORED · NO MEASURED COLOUR SEPARATES THESE FILMS; THIS ONE WAS CHOSEN FOR
THE MAP`, printed every time the world is on screen. In the strip, absent
(finding 6).

**Contrast.** Worst visible type on the wall measured **4.88:1** at 8.5px
against a 4.5:1 floor; nothing on any surface walked came in under it. The
palette work holds. Zero border radius throughout, confirmed.

**Console.** Clean on every route walked, at every viewport, other than the
container's own blocked-image noise.

## Coherence

Nine visual languages are now live at once: five edge hues plus a dash, three
honesty glyphs, 28 register hues, four motion signatures, silver-vs-developed
chroma, a gold ring for loved, the gate/lamp/grain apparatus, the strip's
perforations, and the thread rail's. That is a lot, and the risk at this pace is
accretion rather than ugliness — as expected.

It mostly holds, because almost all of it is disciplined by the same idea. What
does not hold is that **three of those nine are competing for one channel.** At
field scale, colour is asked to carry the register (finding 5), the reader's own
development, and — in the strip — both at once, with the strip's ground being
the reader's plate while its frames are the register's. DESIGN already names
this collision twice (open question 3a, "inside a chosen world the integration
pass is off") and has resolved it by turning things off. That is the right
temporary answer and it will not survive a third claimant.

The bigger coherence problem is quieter. **The insights are not in the picture.**
Claims live in the panel and in Passage. The constellation shows five relation
colours and no words; the map on arrival shows six relationship *names* and, as
DESIGN honestly records, not one claim. Two of the three views are about
structure, and structure is the least surprising thing this corpus knows. If the
insights are why they stay, the insights are currently one surface deep, and
that surface opens on the second gesture.

## The three things I would fix first

1. **The aperture.** Finding 1. It is one line, it is today's regression, it
   silently miscomposes the constellation on every route a reader takes into it,
   and fixing it makes the whole-atlas view look the way it was designed to look
   at zero cost to anything else. Verified by patching the guard out and
   re-rendering. Whatever else happens this week, this should not survive the
   night.

2. **The strip over Passage.** Finding 2. The best prose in the product is under
   the newest chrome at all three viewports, and the fix is the same
   one-condition edit that already governs the strip everywhere else.

3. **The front page's first sort key.** Finding 3. Degree-first ordering on the
   wall *and* in `Surprise me` puts 83% of the corpus behind a search box, and
   it is the direct contradiction of "films they're going to love". This is the
   only one of the three that needs a decision rather than an edit, which is why
   it is third and not first — but it is the one that matters most to where the
   project says it is going.

Runner-up, and close: finding 4, the Passage dim inversion. It is four
characters of work and it fixes a view that currently contradicts the project's
own signature.

## Is the site becoming more or less itself?

**More — decisively, and with one qualification that is starting to bite.** A
year of this project's decisions could be summarised as "refuse the generic
thing and pay for the specific one", and today did that at a rate nobody should
be able to sustain: a gate that is an actual 1.37 Academy aperture fitted to the
band the camera already uses; meteors whose taper is convex because a straight
cone rasterises to a stub; a develop that moves chroma and provably not
luminance so that marking a film cannot add one photon to the atlas; a colour
tier system that prints *"no measured colour separates these films; this one was
chosen for the map"* rather than letting a pretty hue imply a finding. Nothing
about that is an AI-built site. It is a made object with opinions, and the
opinions are getting sharper, not softer.

The qualification is that the project is now building faster than it is
*looking*. Every one of findings 1, 2, 4 and 6 is a case where the reasoning was
right, the measurement was right, the note in DESIGN.md was right — and the
thing on screen does not match it, because the check that would have caught it
measured the model rather than the picture. The gate is verified "at three
viewports to 0.000px" in a build gate that calls `skyGate()` directly; nobody
asked the *page* what it drew. The strip is documented as retracting when the
atlas is not whole; nobody opened a Passage and looked. This is a subtler
version of the jsdom trap STATE.md already warns about, and it is the failure
mode a day like today produces: the argument is so good that the render feels
like a formality.

The other thing to say plainly: the atlas is getting more beautiful faster than
it is getting more *personal*. Twenty-eight worlds, a night sky, a re-form, an
address, a gate — and the reader's own presence is forty coloured dots that
nothing on screen explains, reached through two buttons three gestures deep,
counted by a header that says zero. The apparatus of "THEIR atlas" is built and
measured and correct. The invitation is not.

Fix the aperture tonight. Then decide what the front page is for.
